# Changelog

## 0.4.0 — 2026-11-11

- **The hours that are over stay as they were.** Every model run computes the whole day again, the morning included, so the day's total kept moving until the evening and could end far from what the day had brought. An hour that has ended now keeps the value it ended with. With a yield meter, the plant's energy of the day counts the finished hours with what the meter measured in them (attribute `measured_hours`); the planes keep their forecast.
- **Station correction.** While the sun is up, the global radiation the nearest MeteoSwiss measurement station (SwissMetNet, within 25 km and 300 m of height) received in the last hour is set against the forecast. The ratio corrects the running hour and, fading out, the next three. It works without a meter and without a setting; a new diagnostics sensor `…_station_correction` shows it, and a switch under Configure → Σ turns it off. Costs one small request in 15 minutes during daylight, up to 1 MB a day in summer.
- **The calibration learns by the sun's position** instead of by the hour of the day: one factor per part of the sky (15° of direction, 10° of height). Shading learned in autumn keeps fitting in winter and spring, when the sun passes the same tree at another time. What was learned by the hour cannot be carried over: the learning starts anew with this version and needs its two to three weeks again.
- **The yield meter is asked for at the setup**, as an optional field of the first step. It remains an option that can be changed or removed later.
- **Accuracy day by day in the diagnostics**: measured energy, forecast and day-ahead forecast of the last 30 days, with the state of the station correction and of the finished hours. A new issue form, *Forecast accuracy report*, takes the file. Nothing is sent by the integration itself; the station's name is not part of the download.
- The attribute `hourly` of `…_calibration_factor` now lists today's hours; the factors per part of the sky are in the new attribute `sun_position`.

## 0.3.1 — 2026-11-10

- **Fix**: a plant that lies closer to a measurement station or a mountain point than to the centre of its postal code never loaded («has no global radiation for this point», the entry kept retrying). The list of forecast points names stations, mountain points and postal-code centres alike, but only the postal-code centres carry radiation, and the setup took the nearest point of any kind. It now takes the nearest one with radiation, and an entry that sits on a point without it moves over by itself at the next start — nothing to set up again.
- **Docs**: the README no longer suggests picking a mountain point, and no longer lists the snow model as missing.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-11-10

- **Snow on the modules.** A covered module produces nothing, however bright the day. The integration now follows the snow from the forecast itself: precipitation that falls in the cold covers the modules (one millimetre of water, about a centimetre of fresh snow, covers them completely), the snow slides off when the modules warm up — the faster the steeper they stand — and melts with warm air and sun. The forecast of every plane is reduced by the share that is covered. New sensor *Snow on the modules* with the cover in percent, the figure per plane, the snow on the ground, the hour from which the modules are expected to be free and the energy the snow takes today. The state is kept from one model run to the next and across restarts. With a yield meter, modules that produce although the forecast shows them covered are set free at once. Switched on by default, also for existing plants; to switch it off use Configure → Σ. The thresholds are taken from the literature and are an estimate.
- **Forecast accuracy.** With a yield meter, two diagnostics sensors tell how well the forecast did over the last 7 and 30 days: the accuracy of the forecast that stood at the start of each day as the state, and as attributes the accuracy of the last forecast before each hour, the hourly accuracy, the bias, the number of days and the energy forecast and measured. Only days of which nearly every hour was counted enter the figures.
- **Finer physics.** The diffuse light is split into the bright ring around the sun and the rest of the sky (Hay & Davies) instead of an evenly bright sky; the module glass reflects light that falls in flat (Martin & Ruiz); the cell temperature follows the forecast wind (Faiman) instead of a fixed NOCT; behind terrain a plane loses the part of the sky the mountains hide, not only the direct beam; snow on the ground reflects more light onto steep planes. Clear days come out a little higher on planes that face the sun and lower on planes that face away; mornings, evenings and flat roofs a little lower.
- Two more parameters are read from the local forecast for this: precipitation and wind. A run without them still works; the snow sensor then shows *unknown* and the wind counts as a light breeze.
- The self-calibration leaves out the hours with snow on the modules: snow is no property of the plant.
- **Less traffic, and the true figure.** A model run cost about 2.4 MB, not the 300 KB the README named: the temperature file is grouped by timestamp instead of by point, so one point needs a read in each of its 200 blocks. The temperature series is now kept and read again every six hours only — the few hours a newer run adds at its end repeat the day before — while the other four files, about 160 KB together, are read with every run as before. That makes about 13 MB a day instead of 57 MB. The `…_run` sensor shows what the last read cost, and the diagnostics download tells which run every series comes from.
- **Fix**: reconfiguring a plant without typing the address again removed the address from the entry, and the device card showed the name of the forecast point instead. The address now stays for as long as the marker is left where it is; a marker moved elsewhere drops it, and a new search replaces it. Forecast, planes and entity IDs were not affected.
- **Docs**: `SECURITY.md` says what the integration sends to the two federal services and what it stores.
- The diagnostics download carries the snow as it is followed and the accuracy figures.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-09

- **Terrain horizon.** The horizon of the place is read once from the swisstopo elevation model (five requests to the federal geo API, about 2 MB, kept afterwards): 37 directions from north-east over south to north-west, 25 km far. While the sun stands behind a mountain or a valley side, the direct beam of that part of the hour is left out and only the diffuse light counts. Switched on by default, also for existing plants after the update; to switch it off use Configure → Σ. New diagnostics sensor *Terrain horizon* with the highest angle and the whole profile. Bare terrain only: no trees, no buildings.
- **Self-calibration against your own meter.** Name the energy sensor of the plant under Configure → Σ, and every full hour the forecast is compared with what the meter counted. One factor per hour of the day is learned, with a fading memory of about two weeks, and applied to the coming days: shading by trees and buildings, soiling, lasting snow. New diagnostics sensor *Calibration factor* with the factor per hour, the number of learned hours and the energy of today without the calibration. Nothing changes without a meter.
- The energy sensor of today tells in its attributes whether the terrain horizon and the calibration are at work; the diagnostics download carries both in brief, with the name of the meter redacted.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-09

- **First release.** The expected output of a PV module plane from the MeteoSwiss local forecast: global and diffuse radiation and air temperature, hour by hour for nine days, read from the open data with range requests. Sensors for the energy of today, tomorrow, the day after and the rest of today, the power of the current and the next hour, today's peak and its time; the forecast feeds the energy dashboard's solar production forecast. Setup from the map marker or a street address: the roof planes of the building from the Sonnendach solar cadastre (SFOE) with orientation, tilt, area, suitability and long-term yield, ticked from a list and corrected if needed, plus planes entered by hand; the figures of every plane one after the other (peak power, losses, inverter limit, damping for calibration); the forecast point is chosen automatically. One entry is the whole plant: totals for the plant, a sensor per plane, the sum for the energy dashboard. With cadastre roofs, sensors for the typical day of the month and the forecast as a percentage of it. Reconfigure moves the plant; Configure changes the plane. Repairs notice when the data cannot be read, diagnostics download, German, English, French and Italian.
